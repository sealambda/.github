# Write an explanation

An explanation helps the reader to understand a topic: the background, the
reasons, the trade-offs and the alternatives. The reader studies it away from
the keyboard, to connect what they know. The source of this guide is the
Diátaxis page on [explanation](https://diataxis.fr/explanation/). The template
is [templates/explanation.md](../templates/explanation.md).

## What an explanation does

- It covers one topic with a clear edge, named by a noun phrase or by the
  question that it answers.
- It gives the context: the forces, the limits and the design history that
  explains why it is so.
- It states the reasons and compares the alternatives. It may give an opinion,
  and it says whose opinion it is.
- It connects the topic to related ideas, with links.

## What an explanation does not do

- It does not give steps. A how-to guide or a tutorial does that.
- It does not list facts for lookup. The reference does that.
- It does not hold the changelog or the plan of the project. A page describes
  what is true now; a plan is an issue, and the list of changes is in Git.

## Checks before you publish

- State the topic as a question. If the page answers it, the page is done.
- Find each list of facts and each list of steps, and move them to a reference
  page or a how-to guide with a link.
